# Clark & Jno-Charles (2025) - Five Fundamental Threats Framework
## "The Child Labor in Social Media: Kidfluencers, Ethics of Care, and Exploitation"
## Journal of Business Ethics, Vol. 201, pp. 35-62

### Five Fundamental Threats to Children's Rights in Kidfluencing:

---

## 1. Right to Consent (UNCRC)
- Children cannot meaningfully consent to being filmed/posted
- Origin stories often attributed to child but unreliable (Ryan was 5, Vlad was 5, Niki was 3)
- Consent to activity ≠ consent to surrendering privacy, exploitation risks
- **Indicators**: child's age at channel start, whether content appears scripted vs spontaneous

## 2. Right to Privacy
Four sub-dimensions:
- **Likeness privacy**: child's image/personality exposed to internet permanently
- **Physical privacy**: home location, school, daily routines revealed
- **Emotional privacy**: private moments (tantrums, crying, illness) broadcast publicly
- **Reputational damage**: family scandals become associated with child's brand
- **Indicators**: sharing private moments, emotional vulnerability on camera, location disclosure

## 3. Freedom from Economic Exploitation
Exploitation if:
1. Work is age-inappropriate or hazardous (physically or emotionally)
2. Requires substantial hours taking precedence over schooling
3. Blurs line between work and leisure
4. Makes the child THE PRODUCT
5. Does not provide full and fair compensation
- **Indicators**: frequency of uploads, child as central performer, commercial content, brand deals

## 4. Freedom from Harm
- **Physical harm**: injuries during content creation (Ninja Kidz obstacle courses while sick)
- **Psychological harm**: forced to perform when ill, stressed, or unwilling
- **Harassment**: bullying, predatory comments, demands for personal info
- **Indicators**: dangerous stunts, child performing while visibly distressed, prank content targeting child

## 5. Freedom of Expression
- Children cannot manage their own accounts
- Three mechanisms of violation:
  1. **Scripting child's voice**: ghost-writing dialogue for child to say
  2. **Fake endorsements**: paid product ads portrayed as child's organic opinion
  3. **Brand association**: associating child with products they cannot evaluate
- **Indicators**: scripted content, product placement, child reading from script

## Mapping to Our LF Dimensions:

| Our Dimension | Clark 2025 Threat | Specific Indicators |
|---------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Performative** | Economic Exploitation (#3) + Consent (#1) | Child as central performer, scripted content, blurred work/leisure |
| **Emotional bait** | Freedom from Harm (#4) + Privacy (#2) | Emotional vulnerability broadcast, manufactured distress |
| **Narrative conflict** | Freedom from Harm (#4) | Manufactured scenarios, dangerous/stressful situations |
| **Challenge format** | Economic Exploitation (#3) + Harm (#4) | Age-inappropriate tasks, physical demands, competition pressure |
| **Commercial content** | Freedom of Expression (#5) | Fake endorsements, brand association, scripted product promotion |

---

## Key Quotes for Paper:

> "Kidfluencing ventures exploit children if they (1) require work that is age inappropriate or hazardous physically or emotionally, (2) require substantial hours of work that take precedence over schooling, interfere with a child's right to leisure, or blur the line between work and leisure, (3) make the child the product, or (4) do not provide the child full and fair compensation" (p. 46)

> "a 10-year-old creating content for 2-year-olds, by behaving like a 2-year-old, potentially stunts the 10-year-old's growth and maturation" (p. 46, re: Vlad & Niki)

> "the child's consent is not simply for the activity but carries the concurrent effect of consenting to potentially surrendering their rights to privacy and freedom from exploitation" (p. 42)
