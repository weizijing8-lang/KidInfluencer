# Divon et al. (2025) - Children as Concealed Commodities

**Source:** New Media & Society, DOI: 10.1177/14614448241304657
**Authors:** Tom Divon (Hebrew U Jerusalem), Taylor Annabell, Catalina Goanta (Utrecht)
**Method:** Ethnographic and legal analysis of 215 videos from 23 kidfluencers (Israel, NZ, US) on TikTok

## Four Monetisation/Visibility Practices (their coding framework):

1. **Kids as props, brands as playmates** — children used as visual props in branded content
2. **Transactional childhood** — childhood experiences turned into commercial transactions
3. **Aspirational child-ification** — children positioned to embody idealized/aspirational lifestyles
4. **Regulative parenthood** — parents controlling/directing children's participation

## Key Insight:
- Children become "concealed commodities" with varying degrees of (in)visibility
- From "playful participation in branded content" to "embodying idealised notions of childhood for brand visibility"
- TikTok's policies conceal children's role in monetised content

## Relevance to our LFs:
- Their framework supports our "performative" vs "organic" distinction
- "Kids as props" = our commercial_content dimension
- "Transactional childhood" = our challenge/performative dimension
- Adds nuance: even "playful" content can be exploitative if it's for brand visibility
