# Clark & Jno-Charles (2025) - Key Findings for Our Paper

## Citation
Clark, D. R., & Jno-Charles, A. B. (2025). The Child Labor in Social Media: Kidfluencers, Ethics of Care, and Exploitation. *Journal of Business Ethics*, 201, 35–62.

## How They Define Child Labor in Kidfluencing
- Kidfluencing = "social media business in which children serve as primary influencers of audience opinions or behavior"
- They establish kidfluencing as a **new form of child labor** through UNCRC framework
- Key tension: "greater success in the venture actually increases risks to the participating child's rights"
- At low frequencies → appears harmless play
- At high frequencies → "activity habituates and closer approximates labor"

## How They Measure/Operationalize Child Labor
1. **Filming time** (estimated from final video time)
   - Shooting ratios: 5:1 to 400:1 (minutes shot vs. finished)
   - Even 100 min of content/month → ~1000 min shooting → 4.2 hrs/week minimum
   - Does NOT include travel, preparation, waiting time
   
2. **Upload frequency** - videos per week/month over time (Fig. 1 in paper)
   - Ryan's World: 5-6 days/week
   - Tracked monthly content minutes over channel lifetime

3. **Child's role in video** - proportion of video featuring child
   - Ninja Kidz & Vlad/Niki: children in almost entire video
   - Ryan's World: Ryan now only does 1-2 min intros (reduced over time)

4. **Content type indicators:**
   - Birthday celebrations filmed = leisure commodified
   - Scripted vs. spontaneous interactions
   - Child's visible reluctance/distress
   - Surprise competitions (Peyton Myler example)

## Five Fundamental Threats (Their Framework)
From UNCRC articles, they identify threats to:
1. Right to education (filming interferes with school)
2. Right to leisure/play (play becomes work)
3. Right to privacy (entire childhood documented)
4. Right to be protected from exploitation
5. Right to express views/consent

## Commercialization Indicators They Note
- Family production companies (Sunlight Entertainment LLC for Ryan)
- 30 full-time creative staff
- Multiple YouTube channels (21 for Vlad & Niki, 9 for Ryan)
- Brand deals, merchandise, licensing
- $22M-$24M annual earnings (Ryan's World)
- $3-9M per MONTH (Vlad & Niki)
- Luxury purchases (mansions, Ferraris)
- Cross-platform expansion

## Key Methodological Notes
- Qualitative case study (4 cases)
- Used UNCRC as analytical framework
- Examined top 1000 YouTube channels by cumulative views
- Purposeful sampling across genres (unboxing, adventure play, sports, travel)
- Background research: press articles, blogs, discussion boards
- Data: video content, secondary sources, press interviews

## What's Missing (Our Opportunity)
- They do QUALITATIVE analysis only (4 cases)
- No computational/quantitative measurement at scale
- No statistical test of commercialization → labor intensity
- No control group comparison
- No automated detection of commercial signals

## Key Quote for Our Paper
"It is filming time, estimated from final video time, that is the best barometer of a child's work commitment."

"At low frequencies or at early stages, the positive gains of play, personal expression, and developing positive personal representations can make kidfluencing appear at worst harmless and at best positive. However, as kidfluencing proceeds to the point at which it generates significant income, activity habituates and closer approximates labor."
